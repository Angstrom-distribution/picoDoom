# C8 buffers

Status: proposal. The compositor side is not decided, and the path is untested against a compositor and on a device. `make -C doomgeneric/test c8` tests the choice of format, the scaler and the palette handling with a fake compositor.

The engine renders 320x200 frames of 8-bit palette indices. On a display that scans out 8-bit paletted pixels (DRM format C8 with a gamma lookup table as palette), the indices can go to the display unchanged and the palette is loaded into the display. That moves one byte per pixel instead of two or four, and a palette change (the damage and pickup flashes) moves no pixel.

## When it is used

The back end draws C8 `wl_shm` buffers when all of these hold; otherwise it draws RGB565 or XRGB8888 as before:

- `wl_shm` offers `C8`;
- the compositor offers the palette protocol, and the surface has a palette object;
- the output the surface is on scans out C8;
- the compositor reports the surface as exclusive: alone on the output, so that the palette can be loaded in the display;
- `wayland_c8` and `-c8` are not `off`.

The compositor decides exclusive from the geometry of the surface, not from its buffer format, so a window that is tiled, covered by a panel or rotated stays RGB. The format follows the compositor's reports while the game runs: the buffers are made again when the choice changes, for example when a panel appears over a fullscreen game.

## Scaling

The back end scales the picture itself, with the same row and column plan as for RGB buffers, into a buffer of the size of the output. The compositor does not scale: a scaled or cropped surface is not exclusive, so a 320x200 buffer scaled by the compositor would never be shown natively. `-aspect` and `-stretch` therefore apply as in RGB mode. The letterbox bars are index 0, which the engine's palettes keep black, and are never written after the buffer is made. During a palette flash that tints index 0 the bars tint too.

## Palette

The palette is the engine's, after its gamma table, sent as 256 entries of `0x00RRGGBB` before the commit that needs it. It is sent again only when it differs from the last one sent, and after every change of buffer format. When nothing else changed, a palette change on an exclusive surface is a commit without a new buffer. On a surface that is not exclusive the compositor reads the buffer through the palette, so the commit damages the whole buffer.

## Not done

- Rotated outputs: a rotated output is not exclusive for this surface, so the game draws RGB there. Drawing pre-rotated C8 buffers needs a rotating index scaler.
- A palette per output when the surface is on two.
- Measurements: the saving in time and bus traffic is not measured.
